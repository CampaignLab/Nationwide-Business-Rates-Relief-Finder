TAKE IT TO YOUR HIGH STREET

Ways to use this data

From April 2027, more small businesses could get full relief from business rates. Most haven't heard. For some, it is worth several thousand pounds a year. For others who already get partial relief, it is less. Either way, you can be the one who tells them. Here's how.

THESE BUSINESSES MAY BE ELIGIBLE. THAT IS NOT CONFIRMED.

Every property on the list is a possible candidate, picked out automatically from recorded property information. Being on the list does not mean a business qualifies, that it will pay nothing, or that the current occupier is the one named. The policy has been announced but is not yet in regulations. Always point businesses to their council to confirm.

Before you start

  1. Download your constituency's list. Find your seat on the finder and download the CSV or Excel file. It contains two lists: likely candidates and possible candidates that need more checking.
  2. Start with the likely list. These are properties whose recorded use fits the policy with no detected conflict. Treat the possible list as a second wave, after you have checked the cases.
  3. Check the property is still trading. Records can be out of date. A quick walk past, a web search or a look at the shopfront will tell you if the business has closed or changed.
  4. Plan your timing. The change starts on 1 April 2027 and new bills usually go out in February and March. Contact before then gives businesses time to check with their council.
  5. Decide how you will record what you learn. Note who you contacted, what they said, and anything the list got wrong. Tell us about errors so we can fix them (see the end of this page).

Send a letter to the businesses

The most direct way to reach every business on your list. Every property has an address, so your letter lands on the shop counter, not in a spam folder.

  • Address it to "The business owner" at the property address. The firm names in the data are often missing, out of date or not the legal ratepayer.
  • Keep it short: what is changing, why their property might be affected, and how to check with the council. Include the council's business rates contact.
  • Use "may be eligible" wording throughout. Do not tell anyone they will pay nothing.
  • Offer a way to get in touch if they want help, such as a surgery date or a phone number.
  • If you are an MP or councillor, check the rules on using official stationery and allowances for this kind of mailing.

A suggested letter is in Wording you can use.

Leaflet and visit the businesses

Nothing beats a conversation on the high street. Many listed properties sit on the same parades and town centres, so one walking route can cover dozens of them.

  • Sort the download by postcode or street to plan routes that cover a parade or town centre in one go.
  • Hand a short leaflet to staff or the owner, or drop it through the door if closed. Visit in quieter hours: mid-morning for shops and cafes, before evening service for pubs and restaurants.
  • Talking in person lets you check the business is still there, whether the owner already knows, and whether they have other properties (which can affect eligibility).
  • Volunteers can use the list as a canvass sheet. Record outcomes as you go.

Work with local business groups

Local business groups already have the trust of small businesses. Get them sharing it and you reach far more owners than you could alone.

  • Chambers of commerce: ask them to include it in their newsletter or member briefings.
  • Federation of Small Businesses (FSB) local branches: offer to speak at a branch meeting.
  • Business Improvement Districts and town centre managers: they know every business on their patch and often run regular mailings.
  • Trade groups and local networks: pub, hospitality and independent retail groups, market traders and high street WhatsApp groups.

Share the headline numbers for your area and a link to the finder. You usually do not need to pass on the property-level list; let each business check its own position with the council.

Run Facebook and Instagram ads

Reach the owners who don't open their post or read the local paper.

  • Target by location: a small radius around the high streets and parades where your listed properties cluster.
  • Narrow the audience using interests and job titles linked to small business ownership, retail, hospitality and leisure.
  • Send people to a page that explains the change and tells them to check with the council, not a page that promises savings.
  • Ads from a party, candidate or campaign about this are likely to count as political or social issue ads. Meta requires you to complete its authorisation process and add a "Paid for by" disclaimer before they run.
  • Do not upload the property list to an ad platform as a custom audience. It holds addresses, not contact details, and the VOA data terms restrict how it can be used.

Work with the council

Only the council can confirm eligibility and award relief, so get it on side early.

  • Councillors can ask the council's business rates team how it will tell eligible businesses about the change, and whether it will apply the relief automatically.
  • Share the number of possible candidates in the area with the economic development or town centre team.
  • Ask for the council's business rates contact details to include in your letters and leaflets.

Local press and social media

A local number makes a strong story.

  • Use your constituency's count as a local hook, for example "around [number] properties in [area] may be eligible for full relief from April 2027".
  • Always say "may be eligible" and explain that businesses should check with the council.
  • Feature a real local business that has checked and confirmed it qualifies, with their permission.
  • Do not name individual businesses as eligible without their agreement.

Rules to follow

This is not legal advice. Check the rules that apply to your organisation and your activity.

  • Accurate claims. Say "may be eligible". Do not promise relief, a £0 bill, or a saving. Do not describe the list as a confirmed or official mailing list.
  • VOA data terms. The property data comes from the Valuation Office Agency under a restricted licence, not the Open Government Licence. Read the VOA terms and our data-use notes, and keep the attribution and disclaimer when you share extracts.
  • Data protection. Some ratepayers are sole traders, so their details can be personal data under UK GDPR. Write to the business at the property rather than to a named person, and handle any notes you keep about conversations carefully.
  • Emails and texts. Unsolicited marketing emails and texts to sole traders and partnerships need consent under PECR. Post and in-person contact are simpler.
  • Imprints. Printed and digital material from a party, candidate or campaigner may need an imprint saying who published it. See the Electoral Commission's guidance on imprints.

Wording you can use

Adapt these to your voice. Keep the "may be eligible" framing and the pointer to the council.

SHORT VERSION

"This property may qualify for expanded relief. Please check your circumstances with your council."

LETTER

Dear business owner,

From 1 April 2027, the government plans to give more small retail, hospitality and leisure businesses in England full small business rates relief. Properties with a rateable value above £12,000 and up to £18,000 could see their business rates bill reduced.

Public property records suggest your premises at [address] may be in this group. We cannot confirm that you qualify. That depends on how the property is used, any other properties you occupy, and the final rules.

To check, contact [council name]'s business rates team at [contact details]. If you would like help, [contact details or surgery date].

Yours sincerely,
[Name]

LEAFLET OR AD

"Run a shop, cafe, pub, salon or gym? From April 2027 more small businesses could get full business rates relief. Check whether you may be eligible with [council name]."
